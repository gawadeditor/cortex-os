# Input Reference — EP 001

- **Trial identifier:** `ep_001`
- **Topic:** The Antikythera Mechanism: Who Really Built the Future?
- **Central question:** Was the Antikythera Mechanism a one-off miracle of ancient genius, or the last surviving artefact of an entire engineering tradition that was deliberately or accidentally erased?
- **Central answer (must not be spoiled early):** Leading scholars now argue the mechanism was not an isolated anomaly but the product of a broader Greek mechanical tradition — likely originating with Archimedes and centred on Rhodes — whose other artefacts and knowledge were lost to the collapse of Hellenistic patronage networks and the disruptions of Roman conquest.

## Narrative Arc (must be preserved)

- **Setup:** The Hook and Accepted Explanation establish the dominant framing: a singular, impossible ancient computer found by chance in a shipwreck. The viewer is loaded with awe and the assumption that this was one genius's miracle. The discovery story (sponge divers, Stais's recognition of the gear) grounds the mystery in a human moment.
- **Escalation:** Evidence Against systematically dismantles the lone-genius narrative. The anonymous builder, the 150-year dating window, and Hipparchus's embedded astronomy all point away from a solo act and toward something larger. Each piece of evidence removes a pillar of the accepted story, leaving the viewer without a framework.
- **Revelation:** New Framework delivers the reframe: the mechanism was the product of a documented Greek mechanical tradition, likely centred on Rhodes, supported by a 2021 Nature paper. The 'singular anomaly' is replaced by 'last survivor.' This is the central reveal — it answers the central question and recontextualises every fact presented so far.
- **Resolution:** Deeper Mystery and Reframing refuse to let the story settle. The tradition's disappearance is an open wound — the thousand-year silence, the imperfect gears of real engineers, the unanswered question of whether the erasure was accidental or structural. The viewer leaves not with closure but with a larger mystery: what else has been lost?

## Section Structure (order and purpose must be preserved)

- **Hook** — Open with the accepted framing as if it is truth: the Antikythera Mechanism is a singular, impossible object — an ancient computer that shouldn't exist. Establish the shock of its complexity and the thousand-year silence that followed. The viewer must feel the weight of the anomaly before we challenge it. (tension: high)
- **Accepted Explanation** — Steel-man the popular narrative: a lone genius (possibly Archimedes) built an impossible device; it sank with a Roman-era cargo ship; sponge divers found it in 1900; for a century scholars struggled to believe what they were seeing. Present the discovery story (Stais recognising the gear in 1902) and the device's staggering functions (eclipse prediction, Saros cycle, Olympic tracking). Let the viewer settle into the 'singular miracle' frame — this is the version they already know. (tension: medium)
- **Evidence Against** — Begin dismantling the 'singular miracle' frame. First: the builder is completely anonymous — no maker's mark, no workshop stamp, no city of origin. If this were a lone genius's masterwork, why is there no name? Second: the dating window spans 150 years (205 BCE to 50 BCE), meaning we cannot even place it in a single generation. Third: the lunar gearing encodes Hipparchus's advanced lunar anomaly theory — this is not one person's invention but the integration of an astronomer's theoretical work into mechanical form. The device is a collaboration across disciplines, not a solo act. (tension: high)
- **New Framework** — Deliver the central reframe. Multiple scholars — including Tony Freeth's UCL team in a 2021 Nature paper — argue the mechanism was not an anomaly but the surviving example of a broader Greek tradition of sophisticated gearwork. Rhodes emerges as the leading candidate for manufacture: inscriptions, dialect analysis, and the island's documented scientific tradition all point there. This was not one genius in a workshop — it was a school, a tradition, a lineage of mechanicians who built devices like this as a matter of course. The mechanism is not the miracle. The miracle is that even one survived. (tension: high)
- **Deeper Mystery** — Escalate into the question the reframe opens: why did the tradition vanish? The thousand-year gap is real — no comparable geared mechanism appears in the archaeological record for over a millennium. The research identifies candidate causes (Roman appropriation, collapse of Hellenistic patronage, destruction of knowledge centres) but cannot resolve which was decisive. This is an honest open question. Humanise the builders with the 2026 gear-error finding: even these brilliant mechanicians made imperfect gears that may have jammed. They were not gods — they were engineers whose entire world disappeared. (tension: high)
- **Reframing** — Land the emotional and intellectual payoff. The Antikythera Mechanism is not evidence that one ancient genius was impossibly ahead of their time. It is evidence that an entire civilisation's engineering capability was erased so thoroughly that when we found the last trace of it, we couldn't believe it was real. The thousand-year silence is not a mystery about one object — it is a mystery about what else we have lost. Close with the open questions: Cicero may have seen a working orrery looted from Syracuse; late-antique authors may have described similar devices; Islamic-era instruments may partially bridge the gap. The story is not over. (tension: medium)
