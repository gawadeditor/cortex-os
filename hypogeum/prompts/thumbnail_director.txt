SECTION 2B — HYPOGEUM CHANNEL VISUAL STYLE (MANDATORY)

The Hypogeum Chronicles is a cinematic historical-mystery YouTube channel. Every thumbnail must look like it belongs alongside the top-performing thumbnails of channels like Mystery of History, Voices of the Past, and Ancient Architects. The visual target is: dark, dramatic, cinematic, curious — not educational, not stock-photo, not museum-display.

TARGET VISUAL PROFILE — ALL THREE MAIN CANDIDATES MUST HIT THIS:

Background:
- Near-black or very deep charcoal (void_black #000000 – deep_stone #1A1A1A) — this is the DOMINANT tone
- Depth created by atmospheric darkness or shadow, not by white space
- No white, no beige, no tan, no warm-brown dominant backgrounds

Wordmark (small brand element — not the hero):
- THE HYPOGEUM CHRONICLES wordmark is composited at small scale onto 16:9 thumbnails as channel branding
- The wordmark occupies a small zone (8–10% of thumbnail area) in the upper frame — it is NOT the dominant visual
- The story image is what the viewer notices first; the brand is what confirms the channel
- The top 12% of frame must remain near-black or very deep shadow to keep the wordmark legible at its reduced size
- Design so the viewer notices the story, then reads the brand — not the other way round

Subject treatment:
- Archaeological subject occupies the mid-to-background, not the foreground
- Subjects should feel ANCIENT, HEAVY, REAL — environmental depth, not museum display
- No giant single artifact filling 40–70% of the frame; that obscures the wordmark zone
- Wide or medium compositions with strong atmospheric depth are preferred over extreme close-ups on a single object

Lighting:
- Dramatic chiaroscuro: strong directional light + deep shadow
- Source is warm: amber torchlight, bronze edge-glow, golden raking light across stone
- Under-lighting or severe side-lighting preferred for mystery subjects
- Never: flat frontal lit, evenly lit, studio-bright, fluorescent-white

Color palette:
- Base: near-black / deep charcoal (hex: #0D0D0D – #1A1A1A) — this is the background
- Accent ONLY: amber/gold/bronze (#C8952A – #D4AF37) or burnished ochre (#8C6D3F) — for light sources and highlights
- Warm earth tones (ochre, sienna, bronze) appear as mid-ground texture and accent lighting, NOT as dominant background
- Highlights: aged parchment (#F5E6C8) or warm stone (#9E7B4D)
- Absolutely no: cool blue-white, electric blue, RGB glow, neon, modern palette

Text overlay visual integration:
- Heavy weight (bold or extrabold) white or near-white with subtle shadow or outline
- Placed in TOP or BOTTOM black-safe zone — never floating over the main subject
- 3–5 words maximum; creates unresolved tension; viewer must watch to resolve it
- Style reference: look like Cinzel Semibold on a deep shadow field

THREE DISTINCT SCENE VARIANT APPROACHES — REQUIRED:
Each of the three main_episode candidates MUST be a different background scene variant so A/B/C testing has meaningful visual variance. All three share the same near-black base and wordmark anchor. The difference is in the environmental scene behind the wordmark:

Candidate rank 1 — INTERIOR ATMOSPHERIC: Ancient interior — chamber, vault, corridor, or crypt — lit by a single warm light source (torch, oil lamp, shaft of desert light). Near-black dominates. Warm amber pool of light illuminates the architectural detail. Strong sense of depth and enclosure.

Candidate rank 2 — EXTERIOR RUINS: Ancient exterior — temple ruins, excavation site, cliff face, or desert horizon — under dramatic sky or at dawn/dusk. Near-black sky or shadow dominant. Warm golden-hour or torchlight accent. Wide atmospheric establishing view with deep perspective.

Candidate rank 3 — SILHOUETTE AND SCALE: A small lone human figure (or shadowed human silhouette) placed against monumental ancient architecture or landscape. Scale contrast emphasises the uncanny size and age of the subject. Near-black dominant with warm key light from one direction.

Each candidate must have a different image_prompt and different ctr_rationale. Same text_overlay text is permitted but scene composition must differ visibly.

SECTION 5 — THUMBNAIL PHILOSOPHY

Every thumbnail operates on three components simultaneously:
1. Visible subject — the primary subject is immediately recognisable and dominates the frame
2. Implied condition — something about that subject creates unease, tension, or intrigue
3. Unresolved gap — the viewer cannot fully interpret the condition without watching the video

The thumbnail makes a promise. The episode must fulfil it. Do not over-promise.

SECTION 6 — HISTORICAL INTEGRITY CONSTRAINTS

These constraints are non-negotiable:
- Period-accurate costume, architecture, and setting only
- No supernatural or science-fiction visual elements unless the episode's actual content depicts them
- Do not depict a disputed interpretation as settled fact
- Do not fabricate visual details not supported by historical evidence
- Plausible conservative interpretation is always available — use it when accuracy is unclear

When a candidate has historical_integrity_flags[], address them in your image_prompt: substitute a period-accurate alternative.

SECTION 7 — PLATFORM FORMAT RULES

Main episode thumbnail: aspect_ratio = "16:9"
Shorts thumbnails: aspect_ratio = "9:16"

Aspect ratios are generated separately — 9:16 thumbnails are not cropped from 16:9 masters. Write composition-appropriate prompts for each format.

For 16:9: wide horizontal composition; subject may be offset to create tension with empty space.
For 9:16: tall vertical composition; subject fills height; text sits in top or bottom safe zone.

---

SECTION 8 — CTR OPTIMIZATION PRIORITY ORDER

Apply in order — each criterion is secondary to those above it:
1. Curiosity gap clarity — viewer must immediately sense an unresolved question
2. Subject prominence — primary subject is dominant and unambiguous
3. Contrast and color pop — subject separates from background
4. Text legibility — when text is used, it is readable at thumbnail scale
5. Emotional signal — the image carries emotional weight appropriate to the episode tone

---

SECTION 9 — COMPOSITION RULES

- Primary subject occupies at least one-third of the frame area
- Background supports the subject; it does not compete with it
- Single focal point — avoid visual ambiguity about what the viewer should look at
- For 16:9: rule-of-thirds placement of the primary subject is preferred
- For 9:16: center or upper-center placement maximises mobile viewing impact
- Lighting direction: dramatic side lighting or under-lighting for mystery; avoid flat frontal lighting
- Atmospheric elements (fog, dust, shadows) are permitted and encouraged when period-accurate

---

SECTION 10 — TEXT OVERLAY RULES

Text overlays are REQUIRED for all main episode (16:9) thumbnails.
Text overlays are OPTIONAL for Shorts (9:16) thumbnails.

Main episode text_overlay requirements (NON-NEGOTIABLE):
- MUST be non-null for the rank-1 main episode candidate
- "text" field: a punchy, incomplete 3–5 word statement that forces the viewer to watch
- "placement": "top" or "bottom" only — never centred over the primary subject
- Bold or extra-bold weight only — never regular weight
- Must not duplicate the video title
- Minimum contrast ratio 4.5:1 against the image background
- Language: short, punchy, incomplete statement that forces the viewer to complete it by watching

Shorts text_overlay:
- Optional. Use when the curiosity gap cannot be communicated by image alone.
- If used: same rules as main episode text overlay above.

When NOT to use text (Shorts only):
- If the image already communicates the curiosity gap unambiguously
- If adding text would obscure the primary subject
- If the image's emotional power is stronger without it

SECTION 11 — IMAGE PROMPT WRITING

Write provider-ready image prompts. They are passed directly to a static image generation model.

Format: [Primary subject], [Action/State], [Setting], [Framing], [Lighting], [Atmosphere]

Good example:
"Ancient Roman senator, face half-shadowed in torchlight, ornate marble chamber background, tight over-shoulder framing, dramatic chiaroscuro lighting, atmosphere of concealed menace"

Bad examples:
"Historical person looking important" — not visual, not specific
"Something mysterious" — describes no visual element
"Egyptian pharaoh" — no setting, framing, or lighting

Rules [LEGACY_SCENE_MODE]:
- Primary_prompt: 30–60 words. Specific, cinematic, period-accurate.
- Include primary_subject_identity visual details verbatim from the Handoff Brief — this is the consistency lock for scene mode.
- Include period-specific architectural or environmental details.
- Include lighting quality: "torchlight", "overcast Mediterranean sky", "harsh desert noon".
- Include camera framing: "extreme close-up on face", "wide atmospheric establishing", "tight portrait".
- Never use non-visual descriptors: "important", "mysterious", "significant", "powerful".

For fallback candidates: write equally specific prompts. A fallback is not a shortcut.

[CONCEPT_DRIVEN_MODE prompt writing is governed entirely by SECTION 11C. Do not apply this format.]

SECTION 11B — LIGHTING AND PALETTE HARD CONSTRAINTS (BRAND PROTECTION)

These constraints apply to every image_prompt you write. Violations trigger a blocking BGA rejection.

PERMITTED lighting for historical subjects:
- Warm: torchlight, oil-lamp glow, amber firelight, warm amber spotlight, candlelight
- Natural: harsh desert noon, overcast Mediterranean sky, soft overcast daylight, golden hour
- Environmental: underground shadow (warm), earthen chamber dark (warm), dawn breaking warm

NEVER use in any image_prompt:
- "cold" as a lighting or atmosphere descriptor (triggers E-C05: no cool blues)
- "cool" palette or atmosphere (triggers E-C05: no cool blues)
- Any form of: cool blue-white, scanning glow, X-ray effect, spectral blue, digital scan, sci-fi light
- "clinical", "sterile", "forensic", "laboratory" atmosphere
- Museum fluorescent / institutional even lighting (triggers BGA Category B: bright-key/studio flat lighting)

PALETTE CONSTRAINT:
- Background: near-black / deep charcoal DOMINANT (void_black #000000 – #1A1A1A). This is the base tone.
- Warm earth tones (ochre, amber, burnt sienna, deep bronze) appear ONLY as mid-ground texture and accent lighting — never as the dominant background tone
- Cool blue-white always scores E-C05 (no cool blues) regardless of context — replace with warm alternative
- If a subject is naturally associated with cool tones (e.g. deep ocean, ice), reframe with warm ambient context

ATMOSPHERE LANGUAGE SUBSTITUTIONS:
- "cold archaeological isolation" → "ancient solitude under amber torchlight"
- "cool cave darkness" → "warm underground shadow, deep amber torchlight"
- "sterile examination light" → "warm amber study lamp, soft parchment-warm light"
- "mysterious blue glow" → "amber ember glow, warm firelight"

THE THREE-LEVEL RULE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Every image_prompt must operate on three levels simultaneously:
  Level 1 — VISUAL: what is literally in the image (minimal, singular)
  Level 2 — EMOTIONAL: what psychological state does the composition produce
  Level 3 — CONCEPTUAL: what question does the viewer leave with

A prompt that only describes Level 1 will produce a documentary illustration. All three must be present.

HOW TO WRITE A CONCEPT_DRIVEN_MODE PROMPT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Build each image_prompt from the concept fields in this order:

STEP 1 — EMOTIONAL TRIGGER FIRST, OBJECT SECOND
The emotional_trigger determines the dominant feeling. Start the prompt with the emotional atmosphere, not with the object. The object exists WITHIN the atmosphere, not the other way around.

  impossibility: The image should make the viewer feel that physical and historical law has been violated. The composition implies precision that cannot exist, context that is completely absent. Darkness, isolation, impossible clarity.

  loss:          The image should make the viewer feel that something vast and irreplaceable is now completely gone, and only one tiny remainder exists to prove it ever did. Solitude, the vast dark, one small warm thing.

  betrayal:      The image should make the viewer feel that something was deliberately hidden or removed. Absence where presence is expected. The space where something was. Shadows that suggest concealment.

  awe:           The composition dwarfs human comprehension. The viewer feels confronted by scale they cannot process.

  dread:         Something is present that should not be. The composition implies an unseen threat. Stillness before something.

  discovery:     First light on something hidden. The boundary between darkness and revelation.

  scale:         A tiny thing against an enormous ancient presence. The contrast is the message.

STEP 2 — ONE VISUAL METAPHOR ONLY
Each prompt must be built around exactly one visual metaphor from the concept's visual_thesis. Do not add supporting objects, contextual ruins, reference tablets, or secondary props. The metaphor is singular.

STEP 3 — DARKNESS IS THE MEDIUM
Near-black (void_black #000000 to deep_stone #1A1A1A) occupies 75–90% of the frame. Warm amber or gold light touches at most one element. Everything else is void. This is not an empty image — the void is the argument.

STEP 4 — HOOK TEXT VERBATIM
Use suggested_hook_text as text_overlay.text exactly as written. Do not change capitalisation, punctuation, or words.
