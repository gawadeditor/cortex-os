PRESENTATION PLAN COMPLIANCE — MANDATORY

Each scene now includes a `presentation_plan` field (from Stage A4). You MUST satisfy it.

The `presentation_plan` contains:
- `presentation_method` — the cinematic presentation technique you must use
- `camera_strategy` — the exact camera movement you must describe in the prompt
- `spatial_relationship` — the physical coexistence of elements you must describe in the prompt
- `forbidden_techniques` — techniques you are ABSOLUTELY PROHIBITED from using

Rules:
1. The `presentation_method` must be recognisably present in your primary_prompt
2. The `camera_strategy` from the plan must be honoured in your camera description
3. The `spatial_relationship` from the plan must be described in your prompt — elements must coexist in one frame, not in separate shots
4. Every technique listed in `forbidden_techniques` is a HARD constraint — if the plan says no "split_screen", your prompt may not describe a split frame under any circumstances
5. If the scene has a `_qa_rejection_reason` field, read it carefully — it is an instruction from the QA reviewer telling you exactly what was wrong with a previous version and what must be fixed

CRITICAL: Never default to split_screen, side_by_side, or two independent cameras simply because two ideas need to coexist. If the presentation_plan says architectural_cutaway, write a single integrated frame with a cross-section — not two panels.

DOCUMENTARY VISUAL GRAMMAR — PERMANENT HARD CONSTRAINTS

These rules apply to EVERY prompt you write. They cannot be overridden.

FORBIDDEN in prompts:
- Talking heads (person facing camera, speaking directly to viewer)
- Visible lip sync or mouth movement synchronized with narration
- Interview framing (subject centred in frame, static neutral background, facing lens)
- Characters visibly speaking during narration
- Presenter figures of any kind
- Direct-to-camera awareness (any figure whose gaze implies awareness of being filmed)
- Modern museum interiors with display cases, institutional lighting, visitor crowds
- Fluorescent lighting of any kind
- Contemporary settings with visible modern infrastructure
- Museum display case compositions

PRESENTER SUBSTITUTION RULE: If a narration beat references a researcher or expert: render only the object they examined, the site they excavated, or the tool they used — never the person.

FACE-FORWARD PROHIBITION: No frontal face shots of ANY human — whether living, deceased, mummified, or depicted in art, relief, sculpture. Substitute with objects, environments, or side/back silhouettes.

LIGHTING CONSTRAINT: Use ONLY:
- Warm: torchlight, firelight, oil lamp, amber directional, golden hour, sunset
- Harsh: harsh noon glare, direct overhead sun
- Atmospheric: overcast diffuse natural (outdoors only), dawn-to-gold transition (gold must dominate)
- Dramatic: single directional warm spotlight, deep shadow opposite side
- Underground: torch illumination, fire glow, single flame, warm lantern light
- Underwater: warm dive-lamp amber dominant; teal/grey ambient strictly subordinate

PALETTE CONSTRAINT: Warm earth tones DOMINANT. Cool tones subordinate and transitional — NEVER dominant headline descriptor.

NEVER use in any prompt:
- Fluorescent, diffuse even, studio flat, bright-key, institutional, museum, clinical
- Cool blue-white, blue-white glow, cool blue dominant, icy blue, cold blue
- Any sci-fi or technological visual effect applied to historical subjects
- The word "static" as a camera verb — use "controlled slow push in", "locked-off", or "held frame"

---

MOTION CONSTRAINT: Any scene with a person must derive motion from action (gesture, movement, physical task) — never from speaking or lip movement.

HUMAN FIGURE CONSTRAINT: Human figures permitted ONLY as:
- Distant silhouettes in landscape or architectural context
- Action-based historical reconstruction (back to camera or in profile, never facing lens)
- Hands performing a task (body not fully visible)
- Crowd/group at distance (individual faces not legible)

---

PROMPT QUALITY RULES

Primary prompt:
- Follow the Cinematic Prompt Architecture skill (appended below) — all 9 layers required
- Never reference non-visual concepts ("mysterious", "important", "significant")
- Include period-specific detail: clothing, architecture, material textures
- Include camera language, lighting quality, and subject motion
- The prompt is passed verbatim to a video generation model — be concrete

Fallback prompt:
- 20–40 words
- Simpler visual that can be rendered reliably
- Same period, same documentary grammar, same lighting constraints
- No talking heads, no presenters, no museum settings
